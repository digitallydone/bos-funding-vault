The Canadian Funding Landscape: Where to Start

A practical reference guide for nonprofit leaders, fundraisers, and grant writers who want a stronger starting point for funding research.

Last verified against official source pages on April 24, 2026. This guide focuses on source portals, public funders, and recurring programs worth monitoring. Individual opportunities open and close throughout the year, so always confirm eligibility and current deadlines before applying.
Get the Free Guide Explore Early Access

How to use this guide

  • Start with the sources closest to your mission, geography, and beneficiary group
  • Track each relevant source in one place instead of relying on saved tabs
  • Review recurring deadlines monthly, not only when funding feels urgent
  • Use the guide to build a shortlist before deeper proposal work begins

Federal and national sources

1. Government of Canada grants and funding portal

Best fit: Nonprofits, charities, and community projects across sectors.

Why monitor it: Broad federal starting point for grants and contributions.

Source: canada.ca

2. Employment and Social Development Canada funding

Best fit: Jobs, training, accessibility, youth, seniors, and social development initiatives.

Why monitor it: Strong source for workforce and inclusion-related programming.

Source: ESDC funding

3. Canadian Heritage funding

Best fit: Arts, heritage, sport, official languages, multiculturalism, and local cultural work.

Why monitor it: Useful for festivals, museums, arts groups, and heritage organizations.

Source: Canadian Heritage

4. Canada Council for the Arts

Best fit: Arts organizations, festivals, publishers, and artist-serving nonprofits.

Why monitor it: Essential national arts funding source with multiple streams.

Source: Canada Council

5. Women and Gender Equality Canada

Best fit: Organizations advancing gender equality and serving women, girls, and 2SLGBTQI+ communities.

Why monitor it: Relevant for equity, safety, and leadership initiatives.

Source: WAGE funding

6. New Horizons for Seniors Program

Best fit: Organizations serving older adults and community participation for seniors.

Why monitor it: Good source for inclusion, mentoring, and anti-isolation programs.

Source: New Horizons

7. Enabling Accessibility Fund

Best fit: Organizations improving accessibility in community spaces and workplaces.

Why monitor it: Useful for infrastructure and accessibility upgrades.

Source: Accessibility Fund

8. Building Communities Through Arts and Heritage

Best fit: Community groups, heritage organizations, and local event organizers.

Why monitor it: Strong for place-based cultural and heritage programming.

Source: Building Communities

Regional and provincial sources

9. FedDev Ontario

Best fit: Southern Ontario nonprofits and ecosystem organizations.

Why monitor it: Relevant for innovation, regional growth, and workforce-related initiatives.

Source: FedDev Ontario

10. Atlantic Canada Opportunities Agency

Best fit: Atlantic Canadian community and economic development organizations.

Why monitor it: Strong source for entrepreneurship, skills, tourism, and regional growth.

Source: ACOA

11. PrairiesCan

Best fit: Alberta, Saskatchewan, and Manitoba organizations.

Why monitor it: Supports regional diversification and community development.

Source: PrairiesCan

12. PacifiCan

Best fit: British Columbia nonprofits and regional organizations.

Why monitor it: Includes opportunities tied to innovation and community growth.

Source: PacifiCan

13. Canada Economic Development for Quebec Regions

Best fit: Quebec-based nonprofits and regional support organizations.

Why monitor it: Useful for ecosystem and regional development initiatives.

Source: CED Quebec

14. Canadian Northern Economic Development Agency

Best fit: Yukon, Northwest Territories, and Nunavut organizations.

Why monitor it: Critical for northern and Indigenous community development work.

Source: CanNor

15. Ontario Trillium Foundation

Best fit: Ontario nonprofits, charities, and local impact organizations.

Why monitor it: One of the strongest recurring sources for Ontario-based nonprofits.

Source: Ontario Trillium Foundation

16. Ontario government funding opportunities

Best fit: Ontario-based nonprofits and community organizations.

Why monitor it: Broader starting point for province-led grants across sectors.

Source: Ontario funding

Community and sector-specific sources

17. Community Foundations of Canada network

Best fit: Place-based nonprofits seeking local foundation support.

Why monitor it: Good starting point for identifying community foundations close to your service area.

Source: Community Foundations of Canada

18. Municipal grant programs

Best fit: Organizations delivering local arts, culture, youth, recreation, or neighborhood programming.

Why monitor it: Often overlooked source of smaller recurring support.

Source: Search your city plus “community grants” or “nonprofit grants.”

19. Environment and Climate Change Canada funding

Best fit: Conservation, climate, restoration, and environmental education organizations.

Why monitor it: Relevant for biodiversity, land, water, and stewardship programs.

Source: Environment funding

20. Health Canada funding

Best fit: Community health, prevention, substance use, and public health initiatives.

Why monitor it: Useful for health-equity and prevention-focused work.

Source: Health Canada

21. Public Safety Canada funding programs

Best fit: Crime prevention, resilience, victim support, and community safety initiatives.

Why monitor it: Strong fit for prevention and community resilience programming.

Source: Public Safety Canada

22. SSHRC partnership and community research funding

Best fit: Nonprofits partnering with colleges or universities on community research.

Why monitor it: Valuable when your organization can co-create research or evidence-building work.

Source: SSHRC

23. CIHR funding opportunities

Best fit: Health nonprofits and community-linked research partnerships.

Why monitor it: Helpful for pilots, evidence-building, and community health research.

Source: CIHR

24. Multiculturalism and Anti-Racism Program

Best fit: Organizations focused on anti-racism, inclusion, and intercultural dialogue.

Why monitor it: Important for community-serving nonprofits working across diverse populations.

Source: Anti-Racism Program

25. Canada Cultural Spaces Fund

Best fit: Arts and heritage nonprofits needing space development or infrastructure support.

Why monitor it: Relevant when your organization operates or develops a cultural venue.

Source: Cultural Spaces Fund
